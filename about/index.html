About Dohe

Dohe by ShareYourGroup presents 52 dohe (couplets) of three great poets of Hindi, Rahim, Kabir and Tulsidas, each with the meanings of its difficult words, its meaning in simple Hindi and in English, and a line on what it says to us today.

Where the texts come from

  • Rahim: "Rahim-Kavitavali", edited by Surendranath Tiwari, 1926, read on Hindi Wikisource in November 2026. The number under each doha is its number in that edition.
  • Kabir, first three pages: the text of the "Kabir Granthavali" (after the edition of Shyamsundar Das), read on Hindi Wikisource in November 2026. The online copy has scanning mistakes in places; where it was unclear, the usual Granthavali reading is given.
  • Kabir, fourth page: eight couplets that are recited everywhere in Kabir's name, given in the popular wording. They were not taken from a critical edition, and the page says so.
  • Tulsidas: dohe of the Ramcharitmanas in the commonly printed text, with book and doha number, and a few from the Dohavali and from popular tradition, marked as such.

All three poets died centuries ago and their poems are in the public domain. The meanings, word notes, introductions and guides were written for this site.

Please note

Old poems reach us through many manuscripts, and the wording differs from one book to another. This site is a help for understanding, not a critical edition. For an examination, write the text as it is printed in your own textbook. The Hindi explanations have not yet been read by a second native speaker. If you find a mistake in a text or a meaning, please tell us. Last reviewed in November 2026.
